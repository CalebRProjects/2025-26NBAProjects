# Overview
Peyton Watson has emerged as a clear positive rotation player for the Denver Nuggets in 2025–26. His offensive game has expanded, particularly as a jump shooter, while he has maintained meaningful defensive value despite a heavier role. Over the season, he has averaged roughly 14 points on 50/40/75 shooting splits, and in the nine games without Nikola Jokić he has carried a far larger offensive load while preserving efficiency.

# Purpose
The analysis is designed to answer four questions:

1. How real is Watson’s jump-shooting improvement?
2. Is his growth driven by shot diet, shot quality, or execution?
3. How has his defensive impact changed as his offensive role expanded?
4. What does his play without Jokić reveal about his ceiling and scalability?

The goal is not to crown or dismiss Watson, but to ground his breakout in measurable evidence rather than short stretches or highlight plays.

# Method & Data

* Catch-and-shoot file
  * Tracks shot type (C&S, guarded, unguarded), time share, FG%, and eFG%.
  * Used to measure whether Watson’s jumper improved through accuracy rather than opportunity.

* Shot distance file
  * Breaks attempts and efficiency into zones: restricted area, non-RA paint, midrange, corner threes, and above-the-break threes.
  * Used to separate diet change from outcome difference.

* Defense file (closest defender within 6 feet)
  * Reports frequency defended, opponent FG%, expected FG%, and the gap between them (percentage points).
  * Supplemented by CraftedNBA percentiles (blocks, deflections, versatility, and overall defensive impact).

 # Why This Matters
 Watson’s breakout is a win for Denver on the court... and a complication for Denver in the summer.

He is no longer a cheap developmental wing. He is becoming the type of two-way player teams actively target in restricted free agency. There are theoretical paths to keeping both Watson and Christian Braun under the current CBA, but they are narrow, expensive, and increasingly unlikely. 

His play -- especially without Jokić -- shows he can scale up as a scorer, creator, and transition threat while staying efficient. That is exactly the kind of player other teams will pursue, and it makes his next contract one of the franchise’s most difficult decisions.
